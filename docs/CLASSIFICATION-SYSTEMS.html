Classification Systems Registry

A governed registry for systems that organize human knowledge, their purposes, contexts, structures, and validation state.

PROPOSED FOUNDATION ADDITION

Purpose

This registry distinguishes systems that organize human knowledge by their nature and intended use. A library taxonomy, an educational classification, a research framework, and a semantic representation standard are not treated as interchangeable.

System families

  1. 01 · Philosophical and civilizational systemsAristotle, Al-Farabi, Ibn Khaldun, Bacon, Comte, and other historically situated schemes.
  2. 02 · Educational systemsPrograms, qualifications, curricula, and fields of study, such as ISCED-F.
  3. 03 · Research and development systemsResearch statistics and funding classifications, such as OECD FORD.
  4. 04 · Library and documentary systemsDewey Decimal Classification, Library of Congress Classification, and controlled vocabularies.
  5. 05 · Bibliometric and publication systemsOpenAlex Topics, Scopus ASJC, Web of Science categories, and publication taxonomies.
  6. 06 · Professional and competency systemsOccupations, work roles, tasks, skills, and competency frameworks.
  7. 07 · Semantic representation standardsRDF, OWL, SKOS, and related representation languages or models—not classifications by themselves.
  8. 08 · Collaborative knowledge graphsWikidata and similar broad entity graphs—not equivalent to one disciplinary taxonomy.
  9. 09 · Model-reconstructed systemsGPT, Gemini, Claude, and other model-generated views recorded as hypotheses, not official standards.

Required registry fields

  • System identifier, official and alternative names
  • System family, author or maintaining institution
  • Purpose, intended use, historical and jurisdictional context
  • Version, publication date, source location, retrieval date
  • License or permitted-use status
  • Original hierarchy or graph structure and node identifier rules
  • Language coverage, limitations, and known biases
  • Import and validation status

Preservation rule

Imported structures remain immutable source snapshots. Corrections, translations, mappings, and proposed children are stored in separate layers.

Original source structure ├── source nodes ├── source edges └── source labels Knowledge-OS enrichment ├── normalized labels ├── canonical concept mappings ├── model-inferred relations ├── human-reviewed decisions └── validation evidence

Initial registry sequence

  1. OECD FORD as a compact official research classification.
  2. UNESCO ISCED-F as an educational comparison.
  3. Dewey or Library of Congress as a documentary comparison.
  4. OpenAlex Topics as a large machine-assisted research taxonomy.
  5. One historical system.
  6. A model-reconstructed view.
  7. Additional model views using the same prompt and controlled settings.

This sequence tests different purposes before scaling the graph.
