# KOS Decision Record (KDR)

Decision ID:
KDR-0001

Title:
Canonical Concept as the shared semantic identity

Date:
2026-08-06

Authors:
Project Team

Status:
Accepted

Priority:
Critical

Category:
Ontology

---

## Context

The same human concept appears under different names, codes, and parent categories across historical, academic, research, library, bibliometric, professional, and model-generated classification systems. Treating every occurrence as an independent concept would create duplication and prevent reliable cross-system comparison.

---

## Decision

Knowledge-OS will represent a shared semantic identity as a `CanonicalConcept`. Source-specific classification entries remain separate `ClassificationNode` entities and link to the canonical concept through explicit, provenance-aware mappings.

A canonical concept does not erase source wording, source hierarchy, disputed boundaries, or alternative interpretations.

---

## Rationale

This separation preserves original classification systems while allowing crosswalks, multilingual aliases, duplicate resolution, graph traversal, and dataset generation against a stable semantic identifier.

---

## Assumptions

- Semantic equivalence can be assessed with explicit evidence and confidence.
- Some mappings will remain disputed or provisional.
- A canonical concept may have multiple broader concepts and multiple perspectives.

---

## Alternatives Considered

### Use classification nodes as the only concepts

**Pros:** simpler ingestion.

**Cons:** duplicates concepts across systems and prevents stable crosswalks.

### Merge source nodes directly

**Pros:** fewer graph entities.

**Cons:** destroys provenance and hides disagreements.

### Use labels as identifiers

**Pros:** easy initial implementation.

**Cons:** fails across languages, synonyms, homonyms, and terminology changes.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- Stable multilingual identity.
- Source hierarchies remain intact.
- Cross-system mapping becomes explicit.

### Negative

- Entity resolution becomes a core engineering problem.
- Review is required for ambiguous merges.

### Unknown

- The appropriate granularity for some concepts will evolve through domain testing.

---

## Risks

Incorrect merging may collapse distinct concepts; excessive splitting may preserve duplicates.

---

## Mitigation

Require provenance, mapping type, confidence, review status, and reversible merge decisions.
